Who is Taki Sadik?

I build and defend infrastructure - and, increasingly, the sensors and robots that run on top of it. From triaging security events at a SOC to running self-hosted servers, passive radar nodes, and GPS-denied rovers, I care deeply about owning my stack.

Right now I work as a Data Center Engineer at Microsoft, applying cyber threat intelligence to physical infrastructure. I also serve as an Incident Manager at the Felixous Technology Inc.. Alongside all of that I am building Chakor, an open-source self-hosted AI workspace, and the Canadian Defence Hardware Program - eleven sensor and robotics builds across sky, water, and ground, each mapped to a real requirement from Canada's Defence Drone Initiative and the Army's MINERVA challenges.

I got into IT through surveillance operations and discovered I cared more about the network underneath the cameras than the footage they captured. That curiosity led me to networking, then Linux administration, then cloud, then security - and now to the hardware itself: SDR receivers, microphone arrays, hydrophones, and the rovers and drones that carry them. Today my home runs a 7-node bare-metal Kubernetes cluster, a zero-trust Proxmox cloud, nine self-hosted services, and a workbench of field sensors feeding a multi-domain common operating picture.

I study at the University of Toronto (B.Eng, Information Technology) and Seneca College of Applied Arts and Technology (postgraduate, Cyber/Electronic Operations), and I document everything I learn as hands-on labs: 116 of them so far, each with real commands, diagrams, and measurable outcomes. I believe in learning by doing.

Doing right now: the Canadian Defence Hardware Program

My bench is full. I read Canada's public defence calls - the Defence Drone Initiative (launched July 2026) and the Army's MINERVA challenges - as free requirements documents, and I'm building against them at hobby scale, every step documented:

  • Sky: Passive Aerial Early-Warning Node - receive-only passive radar on a KrakenSDR, scored against an ADS-B ground-truth receiver.
  • Water: Passive Acoustic Vessel Monitor - solar hydrophone node classifying vessels, with AIS ground truth.
  • Ground: Seismic & Acoustic Perimeter Mesh - solar ESP32 LoRa nodes telling vehicles from footsteps at the edge.
  • GPS-Denied Navigation Rover + Convoy Robots on a Degraded Mesh - ROS 2 EKF navigation with the GNSS cut, and convoys that survive bad links.
  • Thermal Search-and-Rescue Drone + Passive Acoustic Drone Detector - the MINERVA Arctic ISR gap and the NRC/DRDC passive counter-drone idea, at hobby scale.
  • Cold-Soak Battery Rig + Secure Robot-Fleet Updates - cold-weather battery data and supply-chain integrity, the two pain points the Army names outright.

Full parts lists, prices, and build guides: Canadian Defence Hardware Program hub.
