Who is Taki Sadik?

Cybersecurity and infrastructure professional in Toronto. I cut SOC alert MTTR by 25%, run a 7-node Kubernetes homelab, and published 116 hands-on IT labs.

I build and defend infrastructure, and, increasingly, the sensors and robots that run on top of it. From triaging security events at a SOC to running self-hosted servers, passive radar nodes, and GPS-denied rovers, I care deeply about owning my stack.

Right now I work as a Data Center Engineer at Microsoft, applying cyber threat intelligence to physical infrastructure. I also work as an IT Support Technician at Amico Group of Companies in Richmond Hill, supporting Active Directory, Entra ID, Exchange Online, Microsoft 365, Cisco Meraki networks, and ERP systems for users across the company. Alongside all of that I am building Chakor, an open-source self-hosted AI workspace, and the Canadian Defence Hardware Program, eleven sensor and robotics builds across sky, water, and ground, plus six detailed build guides written against the measurable specs in Canada's MINERVA challenges and the IDEaS counter-drone sandbox.

I got into IT through surveillance operations and discovered I cared more about the network underneath the cameras than the footage they captured. That curiosity led me to networking, then Linux administration, then cloud, then security, and now to the hardware itself: SDR receivers, microphone arrays, hydrophones, and the rovers and drones that carry them. Today my home runs a 7-node Kubernetes cluster (Ubuntu VMs on Proxmox hosts), a zero-trust Proxmox cloud, nine self-hosted services, and a workbench of field sensors feeding a multi-domain common operating picture.

I study at the University of Toronto (B.Eng, Information Technology) and Seneca College of Applied Arts and Technology (postgraduate, Cyber/Electronic Operations), and I document everything I learn as hands-on labs: 116 of them so far, across nine categories: Linux Fundamentals, Linux Server Administration, Networking, Security, Python Automation, Databases, Cloud & Azure, Containers & Kubernetes, and Version Control & DevOps. Each lab has real commands, diagrams, expected outputs, and validation checks. I believe in learning by doing.
