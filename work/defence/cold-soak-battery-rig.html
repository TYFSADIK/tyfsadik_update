Cold-Soak Battery Characterization Rig

A heated, instrumented drone battery bay soaked in a lab freezer and in real Canadian winter, logging runtime, voltage sag, and failure modes against temperature - and publishing it all as a reproducible dataset.

2026 Defence Hardware In Progress

Overview

LiPo batteries hate the cold, and cold is the default operating condition for a huge share of Canadian defence scenarios. The Army MINERVA challenges say it plainly: bought-in foreign robotic systems have had problems with cold-weather operation, and Canada currently lacks an ISR drone that works in Arctic conditions. The Defence Drone Initiative, launched July 23, 2026, makes low-cost tactical drones a stated national priority.

So this build is not a drone - it is the measurement rig that tells you whether a drone's power system survives the cold. A battery bay on a quadcopter frame carries Kapton heater pads, foam insulation, six temperature sensors, and power monitors. The whole rig goes into a lab freezer (and later, the actual Toronto winter), and every run logs pack voltage, current, accumulated mAh, cell surface temperature, and bay temperature against time.

The deliverable is deliberately boring and valuable: a clean, public dataset of battery behaviour versus temperature, measured on a stated Army pain point, with a full protocol anyone can reproduce. Vendors publish marketing numbers; hobbyists publish anecdotes; a controlled, documented dataset at 0, -10, -20, and -30 °C is rare, easy to explain, and directly useful to anyone sizing an Arctic-capable platform.

How I Built It

01
Instrumented bay

Kapton heater pads are bonded directly to the battery bay of the quadcopter frame with polyimide tape, driven through a logic-level MOSFET on a heatsink, and the whole bay is wrapped in foam insulation so the heat budget is measurable rather than leaking to ambient.

Six DS18B20 digital sensors go where the physics matters: taped to the cell surface, on the bay wall, on the heater pad itself, in ambient air, and spares for cross-checking. On the Raspberry Pi they hang off one 1-Wire bus with a 4.7 kΩ pull-up, read through the w1-therm kernel driver via /sys/bus/w1/devices/.

02
Power logging

Two INA228 power monitors sit in series with the pack and the load, logging bus voltage, shunt current, and accumulated charge (mAh) over I2C at a fixed sample rate. One watches the discharge path; the second watches the heater circuit so heating energy is costed separately from flight energy.

The logger is a small Python service using smbus2, writing timestamped rows to CSV on the Pi's NVMe storage, with the Pico datalogger and Fluke multimeter as independent sanity references on the bench.

03
Freezer protocol

Each run soaks the fully charged pack in the freezer at a fixed setpoint - 0, -10, -20, -30 °C - and waits until the cell-surface sensor (not the air sensor) has stabilized at the target, because the core temperature is what the chemistry feels.

The pack then discharges through a fixed resistive load while the logger captures the full voltage-sag curve, runtime to cutoff, and exactly how the cutoff behaves - graceful sag or a hard collapse. Every run repeats so the dataset carries variance, not single anecdotes.

04
Winter runs

The freezer is a controlled stand-in, not the real thing. The same protocol repeats outdoors through a Toronto winter - porch and balcony runs at whatever the weather gives - to validate the freezer data against reality and catch what a chest freezer misses: wind stripping heat from the bay, humidity, and snow on the insulation.

05
Heater strategies

Three strategies get compared at each temperature: unheated (the baseline), thermostat-controlled (the MOSFET holds the bay at a setpoint with a hysteresis band driven from the pad sensor), and pre-heat-and-fly (heat on the ground, disconnect, fly on stored warmth).

The point of the comparison is an honest energy accounting: the INA228 on the heater circuit measures exactly how many mAh heating costs, so the dataset can say whether heating recovered more capacity than it consumed at each setpoint.

06
Publish

The end product is the dataset itself: per-run CSVs, plotted voltage-sag curves, runtime and failure mode versus temperature, plus the full protocol - setpoints, soak criteria, load value, sensor placement, and code - so anyone with a freezer and fifty dollars of sensors can reproduce or extend it.

Testing & Metrics

The protocol is fixed before the numbers are collected, so the results - whatever they are - mean something:

  • Setpoints: 0, -10, -20, -30 °C, with the soak criterion defined on the cell-surface sensor stabilizing, not the freezer's own thermostat.
  • Runtime to cutoff: discharge at a fixed load from full charge to the pack's cutoff voltage, per temperature, per strategy (unheated / thermostat / pre-heat).
  • Voltage sag curve: the full V(t) trace at each setpoint - how deep the initial sag is under load and whether the pack recovers as discharge self-heats it.
  • Delivered capacity: accumulated mAh per run, reported as a fraction of the room-temperature baseline.
  • Failure mode: graceful sag vs hard cutoff vs heater unable to hold setpoint, logged per run.
  • Heating energy cost: mAh spent on the heater circuit, so strategies are scored on net recovered capacity.

Freezer runs are under way; the plotted dataset is not published yet - nothing here reports measured numbers until the repeated runs and outdoor validation are complete.

Safety & Canada Notes

LiPo safety: a LiPo bag and an ABC extinguisher live at the bench, and the rig is never left unattended while a pack is under load or on the heater.
Never charge a cold or frozen battery: every cold-soak run ends with the pack warming back to room temperature before the charger connects - every run, no exceptions.

Status & Roadmap
