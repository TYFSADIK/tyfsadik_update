Thermal Search-and-Rescue Drone

X500-class quadcopter with a FLIR Lepton thermal camera and a Hailo AI HAT+ running person detection at the edge, pushing georeferenced detections to a live map - the Army MINERVA Arctic ISR requirement at hobby scale.

2026 Defence Hardware In Progress

Overview

The Army's MINERVA challenges say it plainly: Canada currently lacks a drone that can do ISR in Arctic conditions. The Arctic requirement calls specifically for sovereignty operations and search and rescue with the Canadian Rangers. Detecting a person in snow or brush from a cheap thermal camera is the sharpest hobby-scale version of that requirement, and it is what this build does.

The airframe is an X500-class quadcopter with a Pixhawk-class flight controller. A Raspberry Pi 5 companion computer carries the payload brain: a FLIR Lepton 3.5 thermal camera for sensing and a Hailo AI HAT+ running person detection at the edge, so the drone finds people on board rather than streaming raw video back to a ground station. Every detection is fused with the drone's telemetry into a lat/lon point and published to a live situational-awareness map.

The design rule throughout: the drone cues, a human confirms. Nothing beyond detection and notification is automated. In a real SAR sweep with the Rangers, the drone is the wide-area sensor and the person on the ground makes every call.

How I Built It

01
Simulation first

Before any flight hardware was powered, I stood up ArduPilot SITL and Gazebo (both free) on the homelab cluster and developed the mission logic there: lawn-mower search patterns, geofences, and failsafes for link loss, low battery, and geofence breach. This builds directly on the simulation environment from my LENTUS-style drone mapping work.

The payoff is that every behaviour that could cost me an airframe got exercised in Gazebo first - sim_vehicle.py -v ArduCopter against a Gazebo world, with Mission Planner connected as the ground station, exactly as it will be flown for real.

02
Airframe build

I assembled the X500-class kit: frame, motors, ESCs, Pixhawk-class flight controller, GPS, and the ELRS radio link (RadioMaster-class transmitter plus receiver) for control. Props went on last, after bench checks of motor order and direction, and prop guards stay on for any early flight.

ArduPilot on the flight controller handles all stabilization and navigation; the companion computer never touches the control loop directly - it only asks for missions and reads telemetry.

03
Companion computer link

The Pi 5 4 GB talks MAVLink to the flight controller over UART. On the Pi, mavlink-router splits the telemetry stream so the ground station gets its copy while the onboard detection pipeline consumes its own - one serial link, multiple consumers, no conflicts.

This is the same companion-computer pattern ArduPilot documents for Pi-class computers: serial telemetry at 921600 baud, with the Pi receiving attitude, position, and GPS state at a steady rate for the fusion step below.

04
Thermal pipeline

The FLIR Lepton 3.5 (a 256x192-class USB thermal camera is the fallback) streams frames into the Pi. The Hailo AI HAT+ runs person detection at the edge via HailoRT, so inference happens on the drone with no video downlink required.

The model is tuned for thermal signatures in snow and brush - a warm human shape against a cold, low-clutter background is actually an easier detection problem than visible-light SAR, which is exactly why the MINERVA requirement points this way. Detections carry a bounding box and a confidence score.

05
Georeferencing

Each detection is fused with the drone's telemetry at that instant - position, altitude, and gimbal/camera attitude - to project the bounding-box centre into a lat/lon point on the ground. With the camera pointing near-nadir and altitude from the flight controller, a pinhole-camera projection is good enough for cueing a ground team.

06
Live map

Georeferenced detections are published over MQTT to a Leaflet situational-awareness map. Each marker carries its confidence score and a cropped thermal thumbnail, and the planned flight path renders alongside so the operator can see what has been covered.

A human confirms every detection; the system does nothing beyond cueing. In a real search, that map is what the Ranger team lead watches.

07
Compliance gate before flight

The airframe is well over 250 g, so outdoor flight waits on Transport Canada registration and a pilot certificate, plus a NAV CANADA airspace check of the intended test area. Until that gate clears, everything flies in SITL and the payload pipeline gets exercised on bench and ground runs.

Testing & Metrics

The test protocol mirrors how a real SAR sensor would be evaluated, scaled down to what a bench and a backyard allow:

  • Detection performance: recall and false-alarm rate of the thermal person detector against staged targets - a person standing, kneeling, and lying in snow and in brush, at increasing slant ranges. Targets: high recall at short ranges with a false-alarm rate low enough that the human confirmer is not flooded.
  • Georeferencing error: measured distance between reported lat/lon and the target's true GPS position across altitude and off-nadir angle. Target: error small enough to walk a ground team to the marker.
  • End-to-end latency: thermal frame to map marker, measured on the live pipeline. Target: seconds, not minutes.
  • Mission endurance: flight time per LiPo pack at a search-pattern profile, including the companion computer and Hailo load. The cold-weather derating for these packs comes from my cold-soak characterization rig dataset.
  • Failsafe behaviour: link-loss, low-battery, and geofence-breach scenarios verified in SITL first, then in controlled flight after the compliance gate.

Flight-derived numbers are pending: the airframe is still inside the compliance gate, so published results to date are from SITL and bench/ground payload runs. Measured figures will be published here once outdoor testing is lawful and complete.

Legal & Safety Notes (Canada)

Drone rules: anything over 250 g must be registered and flown under a Transport Canada pilot certificate. The X500-class kit is well over that, so flights wait until the certificate, registration, and a NAV CANADA airspace check are done - NAV CANADA's airspace tools come before every flight, not after. Simulation (ArduPilot SITL + Gazebo) carries the early software work.
LiPo safety: packs live in a fire bag, charge only on a balance charger, and are never charged cold or frozen - a pack coming in from the cold warms to room temperature before the charger connects. Same discipline as the cold-soak rig.
